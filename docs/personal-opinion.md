# Personal opinion on the modeling approach (Task 6)

## Experience with the language (UML and OCL)

I have come across UML in other assignments, both during my studies at the university and at work. I believe it is useful because it is easily understood by both technical and non-technical people.

## Experience with the tools

I have the most experience with a tool called UMLet, which is meant for more informal sketching of UML diagrams. For this assignment, I have used a tool called USE, as it supports ternary relations and native OCL constraints and specifications. I also have experience with the Eclipse Modeling Framework, as I have worked with it on my bachelor's thesis, so I could compare the tools I have used.

## Do full UML diagrams make sense in practice?

For really complex systems, a UML diagram that tries to capture the entire system becomes extremely cluttered and unreadable. In my experience, it makes sense to divide the system or scenario we are trying to model into smaller logical components. For example, the C4 model is a good logical separation of software systems.
I would say it also depends on the scenario: whether we are trying to convey an overview of the key components, or to look into a specific business process where we need as much detail as possible.

## When I would and would not use it

I have used UML diagrams to model the architecture of a software system I was working on in a corporate environment. It proved extremely useful, as I was able to express my ideas to business people and other, more senior architects, concisely and without them needing to understand much of the project's context.
I also used it to capture a business process that seemed quite complex from just hearing about it, but became much clearer once it was modeled.
Modeling a system has also helped me notice unwanted patterns and issues in the proposed architecture.

I would not use UML and OCL as a guarantee that the system will work as modeled. Models can oversimplify critical details, which are then lost.

## Final verdict

I believe UML and modeling in general are an incredibly powerful and useful tool, if done correctly.
